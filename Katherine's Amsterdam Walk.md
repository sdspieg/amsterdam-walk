**Stop at the Dock Worker Monument  and give some background and context.**

1. **Jewish History**
   1. Waterlooplein was the center of Jewish life before the war
   2. Outdoor market - one of the only ones opened on Sundays and frequented by many Amsterdammers
   3. Portuguese Synagogue
      1. Built in 1675
      2. Sephardic Jews found their way to Amsterdam from Spain and Portugal - forced to leave and Amsterdam provided religious tolerance and economic opportunity.
      3. Original wooden ceiling and benches
      4. Floor covered with fine sand
      5. No heat or electricity - lit by candles
      6. Lower outer buildings house the world famous oldest still functioning Jewish library in the world (Show Photo Here)
   4. Jewish community grew in the 17th century with Jews from Central and Eastern Europe fleeing pograms
   5. The Great Synagogue across the street was built in the 1670's. It's now the Jewish Historical Museum (Show Photo Here)
      7. During the war the synagogue was closed
      8. During the Hunger Winter of 44-45 wood was taken from the upper galleries and provided the freezing population with heating.
   13. During the 1930's, about 25,000 Jews from Austria and Germany sought refuge in the Netherlands, including the Frank family.
   14. There were about 115,000 Jews in The Netherlands at the start of the war
   15. 85,000 lived in Amsterdam
   16. The Netherlands stayed neutral in WWI. They hoped to remain neutral in WWII as well, but that was not to be.

**2. WWII History - background and context**

   1. Germans invaded Holland May 10, 1940
   2. 5 days of fighting and aerial bombing of Rotterdam - the Dutch surrendered
   3. Initially, very little changed for most of the Dutch population
   4. **Jewish Separation/Isolation**
      1. Government workers had to sign an "Aryan declaration" stating they had no Jewish grandparent
      2. Nov. 1940 - Jews were no longer permitted to work for the government
      3. Jan. 1941 - all persons having any Jewish ancestor had to register with the city government and pay 1 guilder to do so
      4. May 1941, the local Bureau of Statistics was instructed to create a map, putting a black dot on the map which indicated that 10 Jewish people lived there. (Show map here)
   5. **Discrimination**
      5. The Netherlands had a Facsist party called the NSB (National Socialist Movement)
      6. Some party members formed a uniformed unit who, began harassing Jewish citizens and businesses. Violent clashes ensued.
      7. The German occupation police force (Grune Politzei - Green Police) was responsible for dealing with unrest.
      8. Feb., 1941, an NSB man was mortally wounded
      9. Jewish Council was set up in Amsterdam. They were responsible for carrying out anti-Jewish measures set by the Nazi occupiers.
      10. Many young Jewish men, including a boxing team Maccabi formed squads to protect Jewish businesses while NSB uniformed groups continued to march around the city and provoke reactions…
      11. Feb. 19th, a skirmish took place at ice cream parlor Koko. Jewish owners sprayed the Grune Polizei with an ammonia canister, shots by the police were fired and the owners and other Jewish customers were arrested.
   6. **Razzia**
      12. German SS leader Heinrich Himmler along with Hans Rauter, SS chief and head of Police in the Netherlands ordered immediate retaliation
      13. Tried to establish a Jewish Ghetto in the Waterlooplein area complete with barbed wire and raised bridges, but many non-Jews also lived in this area and did not want to leave. Hence, the ghetto was never really successful.
      14. Feb. 22nd and 23rd, the area was sealed off by opening bridges to prevent anyone from fleeing and 427 Jewish men between the ages of 18-35  were rounded up by the Grune Polizei at gunpoint.
      15. They were forced to kneel, hands in the air, suffered various humiliation and then were loaded into vans, beated with rifle butts and were taken to the concentration camp Mauthausen. All but 2 were murdered.
      16. Many non-Jewish Amsterdammers witnessed these scenes as the Sunday market was a buy one.
      17. Local branch of the communist party organized a strike.
      18. They distributed flyers which read, "Strike! Show solidarity with the Jewish part of our society which has been hit so hard."
      19. Tramworkers were the first to strike. Sanitation crews, dockworkers, shipyard worker left their work place to join. Dept. stores and other shops and businesses closed their doors. Teachers and students left their classroom.
      20. About 300,000 people in Amsterdam and other large cities were striking.
      21. The German occupation was taken completely by surprise and responded with force. No strikes in the Third Reich! (Show Photo Here)
      22. 9 participants were killed and many others wounded, arrested, some deported and eventually executed
      23. The strike is the only instance of a general strike in all of occupied Europe protesting the anti-Jewish measures.
      24. This statue, The DockWorker, is a man standing tall and represents the power of the ordinary working class against that of the oppresso.
      25. Each year in February, a memorial service is held here.
1. **Anti-Jewish Measures - Denial of Rights**
   7. March, 1941 - businesses taken over by non-Jews
   8. April, 1941 - Cafe's forbidden to Jews
   9. Sept. 1941 - Jewish children had to attend separate schools
   10. Sept. 1941 - parks, swimming pools, sports fields - all public accommodations were forbidden to Jews.
   11. Jan, 1942 a large J was added to identity cards
   12. May 3rd, 1942, Jews 6 years and older had to wear a yellow star of david on the left side of any visible clothing
      26. 4 stars were made for each Jew in The Netherlands
      27. They had to buy the stars at 4 cents each and turn in a ration ticket for textiles as well.
      28. If you were caught not wearing the star you could be fined 1000 guilders or 6 months in prison. (Show Photo Here)

6. **Auschwitz Monument**
   22. Created by Jan Wolkers, Dutch writer and artist.
   23. Unveiled in 1993 to commemorate the Duch Jews who dies in the extermination camps. The broken mirrors show that the sky has never been undamaged since Auschwitz.
   24. There is an urn containing the ashes of the victims of Auschwitz buried under the mirrors.
   25. When Jan Wolkers created the monument in **1977**, he designed it to be a **field of broken mirrors**, laid flat on the ground, reflecting the sky. The brokenness symbolizes:
      1. The shattering of lives during the Holocaust.
      2. The sky being "broken" because of the horrors that happened — a very poetic idea.
      3. Loss, fragility, and mourning.
